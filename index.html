Origins

1917 to 1958

Before anyone said "anime", Japanese animators were testing the medium in silent shorts, then in a wartime feature, then in a colour film made to rival Disney.

  1. 1917

    The first Japanese animated shorts

    塙凹内名刀之巻 (Namakura Gatana)

    In 1917 Ōten Shimokawa, Seitarō Kitayama and Jun'ichi Kōuchi each released animated shorts, the earliest known in Japan. Most are lost. Kōuchi's two-minute Namakura Gatana, about a samurai and his useless sword, survived: it turned up at an Osaka antique market and was announced in 2008.

    Key facts
    Oldest survivor
    Namakura Gatana (The Dull Sword), by Jun'ichi Kōuchi
    Length
    About two minutes
    Rediscovered
    At an Osaka antique market, announced in 2008
    Who was first
    Disputed, because sources give conflicting premiere dates

    Sources: Wikipedia, Anime News Network

  2. 12 April 1945

    Momotaro, Sacred Sailors

    桃太郎 海の神兵 (Momotarō: Umi no Shinpei)

    Mitsuyo Seo's Momotaro, Sacred Sailors was the first feature-length animated film made in Japan. The Navy commissioned it as wartime propaganda, and it opened four months before the war ended. Osamu Tezuka later said it moved him to tears and made him want to animate.

    Key facts
    Director
    Mitsuyo Seo
    Studio
    Shōchiku Dōga Kenkyūsho
    Length
    74 minutes
    Purpose
    Propaganda ordered by the Navy, aimed at children

    Sources: Wikipedia, Cannes Film Festival, Cubed3

  3. 1958

    Hakujaden, the first colour feature

    白蛇伝 (The Tale of the White Serpent)

    Toei Animation's Hakujaden retold a Chinese legend about a white snake who becomes a woman, and it was Japan's first full-colour animated feature. Toei made it to compete with Disney. A young Hayao Miyazaki saw it and later wrote that it made him choose animation.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Toei Animation
    Director
    Taiji Yabushita
    Based on
    The Chinese Legend of the White Snake
    In the U.S.
    1961, as Panda and the Magic Serpent

    Sources: Den of Geek, Film Threat, Cannes Film Festival

Television

1963 to 1979

TV made animation a weekly job. Budgets were small and schedules were brutal, and the shortcuts crews invented became part of how anime looks.

  1. 1 January 1963

    Astro Boy arrives on television

    鉄腕アトム (Tetsuwan Atom)

    Osamu Tezuka's Astro Boy premiered on Fuji TV on New Year's Day 1963 and is called the first popular anime television series. Mushi Production made it on a tight weekly schedule, with cost-saving shortcuts that became a template for TV anime.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Mushi Production
    Network
    Fuji TV
    Based on
    Osamu Tezuka's manga
    Original run
    1 January 1963 to 31 December 1966

    Source: Wikipedia

  2. 6 October 1965

    Jungle Emperor brings colour to TV

    ジャングル大帝 (Kimba the White Lion)

    Jungle Emperor, known abroad as Kimba the White Lion, was the first full-colour anime TV series. Mushi Production adapted Tezuka's manga with part of the money coming from the American network NBC, and the show reached U.S. screens in 1966.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Mushi Production
    Network
    Fuji TV
    First season
    52 episodes, October 1965 to September 1966
    Partly funded by
    NBC, which distributed an English version from 1966

    Sources: Wikipedia, ICv2

  3. 7 April 1979

    Mobile Suit Gundam

    機動戦士ガンダム (Kidō Senshi Gundam)

    Gundam set its giant robots inside a war story with soldiers on both sides. Low ratings cut the TV run to 43 episodes, but Bandai's Gunpla model kits arrived in 1980 and the series was recut into films from 1981. It grew into one of anime's biggest franchises.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Sunrise
    Director
    Yoshiyuki Tomino
    Original run
    7 April 1979 to 26 January 1980
    Episodes
    43, ended early because of low ratings
    Merchandise
    Bandai's Gunpla kits, from 1980

    Sources: Nostalgia King, Wikipedia

Home Video

1983 to 1988

Videotape opened a second market for anime, and in the same few years a new studio and a very expensive film showed what Japanese animation could be.

  1. December 1983

    Dallos, the first OVA

    ダロス (Darosu)

    Dallos, a science-fiction story about a rebellion on the Moon, was the first original video animation: a series made to be sold on tape instead of aired. It was Mamoru Oshii's first work as an independent director, and the OVA format gave studios more room to spend than television did.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Pierrot
    Director
    Mamoru Oshii
    Format
    Four 30-minute episodes, sold on video
    Release order
    Episode two came out first, in December 1983

    Sources: Wikipedia, Anime News Network, Cartoon Research

  2. 15 June 1985

    Studio Ghibli is founded

    スタジオジブリ (Sutajio Jiburi)

    After the success of Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind in 1984, Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata founded Studio Ghibli with producer Toshio Suzuki, backed by the publisher Tokuma Shoten. Its films would go on to set box-office records and win Oscars.

    Key facts
    Founders
    Hayao Miyazaki, Isao Takahata and Toshio Suzuki
    Backer
    Tokuma Shoten
    Came after
    Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind, 1984
    First Ghibli film
    Castle in the Sky, 1986

    Source: Wikipedia

  3. 16 July 1988

    Akira

    アキラ (Akira)

    Katsuhiro Otomo's Akira cost more than ¥1 billion, the biggest budget for an anime film up to that point. Its overseas releases made it a landmark: the UK video edition from Island Records is credited with starting Britain's anime boom, and U.S. distributors followed.

    Key facts
    Director
    Katsuhiro Otomo
    Budget
    Over ¥1 billion, pooled by the Akira Committee
    Japan
    16 July 1988
    United States
    Christmas Day 1989, wider in 1990

    Source: The Japan Times

Breakthrough

1995 to 2001

In a few years anime became a national and international event: a TV series that sparked public debate, and films that broke records and won awards.

  1. 4 October 1995

    Neon Genesis Evangelion

    新世紀エヴァンゲリオン (Shin Seiki Evangelion)

    Hideaki Anno's Evangelion began on TV Tokyo in October 1995. A giant-robot story about teenage pilots, it became a social phenomenon well beyond anime fans and is credited with pulling the industry out of a slump.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Gainax, with Tatsunoko Production
    Director
    Hideaki Anno
    Network
    TV Tokyo
    Original run
    4 October 1995 to 27 March 1996

    Source: Wikipedia

  2. 12 July 1997

    Princess Mononoke breaks the record

    もののけ姫 (Mononoke-hime)

    Hayao Miyazaki's Princess Mononoke was the most expensive Japanese animated film made up to then, at ¥2.35 billion. It overtook E.T. as the highest-grossing film in Japan's history, and it was the first animated film to win the Japan Academy Prize for Picture of the Year.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Studio Ghibli
    Director
    Hayao Miyazaki
    Budget
    ¥2.35 billion
    United States
    29 October 1999, through Miramax

    Sources: Wikipedia, Yale Film Archive, Nausicaa.Net

  3. 2001

    Spirited Away wins the Oscar

    千と千尋の神隠し (Sen to Chihiro no Kamikakushi)

    Spirited Away opened in Japan in 2001, shared the Golden Bear at the 2002 Berlin International Film Festival, and won the 2003 Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. It was the first hand-drawn, anime and non-English-language film to win that category, and it held Japan's box-office record for almost two decades.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Studio Ghibli
    Director
    Hayao Miyazaki
    Berlin
    Golden Bear, 2002, shared
    Academy Awards
    Best Animated Feature, 75th ceremony, 2003
    Japan record
    Held until Demon Slayer passed it in 2020

    Sources: Wikipedia, Cartoon Brew

Going Digital

2006 to 2016

Hand-painted cels disappeared, streaming arrived, and anime found audiences far beyond Japan.

  1. 14 May 2006

    Crunchyroll launches

    Four Berkeley graduates launched Crunchyroll as a site hosting East Asian shows, many with fan-made subtitles. It later moved to legal licensing deals and grew into a major anime streaming service. Sony completed its purchase of the company in 2021.

    Key facts
    Founders
    Kun Gao, James Lin, Vu Nguyen and Brandon Ooi
    Started as
    A site hosting fan-subtitled shows
    Went legal
    Through licensing deals, with a January 2009 pledge to host only licensed content
    Sold to Sony
    Completed 9 August 2021, for US$1.175 billion

    Source: Wikipedia

  2. 29 September 2013

    The last cel episode airs

    サザエさん (Sazae-san)

    Sazae-san, on air since 1969, was the last Japanese TV anime still made on hand-painted cels. Its final cel episode aired on 29 September 2013, and the show has been fully digital since October. Most big studios had already switched in the 1990s.

    Key facts
    Network
    Fuji TV
    Studio
    Eiken
    On air since
    1969
    Last cel episode
    29 September 2013

    Sources: SoraNews24, Gulf News, Anime News Network

  3. 26 August 2016

    Your Name. takes the world

    君の名は。 (Kimi no Na wa.)

    Makoto Shinkai's Your Name. opened in Japan on 26 August 2016 and became the first anime film not directed by Miyazaki to pass ¥10 billion there. It grossed more than US$350 million worldwide, the highest total for an anime film at that time.

    Key facts
    Studio
    CoMix Wave Films
    Director
    Makoto Shinkai
    Distributor in Japan
    Toho
    Japan record
    First non-Miyazaki anime to pass ¥10 billion

    Sources: Cartoon Brew, Animation World Network

Record Years

2020 to 2025

Cinemas in Japan and abroad kept breaking records, Miyazaki won a second Oscar, and overseas fans now bring in more than half of the market.

  1. 16 October 2020

    Mugen Train tops Japan's box office

    劇場版「鬼滅の刃」無限列車編 (Mugen Ressha-hen)

    Opening in the middle of the pandemic, Demon Slayer: Mugen Train passed Spirited Away in December 2020 to become the highest-grossing film in Japanese history, at ¥32.47 billion at that point. It also became the highest-grossing Japanese film worldwide, at about US$500 million.

    Key facts
    Studio
    ufotable
    Director
    Haruo Sotozaki
    Passed Spirited Away
    December 2020, at ¥32.47 billion
    Worldwide
    About US$506.5 million by December 2022

    Sources: Cartoon Brew, Guinness World Records

  2. 10 March 2024

    The Boy and the Heron wins the Oscar

    君たちはどう生きるか (Kimitachi wa Dō Ikiru ka)

    Hayao Miyazaki's The Boy and the Heron won Best Animated Feature at the 96th Academy Awards on 10 March 2024, his second Oscar after Spirited Away. It was also the first Oscar win for its U.S. distributor, GKIDS.

    Key facts
    Studio
    Studio Ghibli
    Japan release
    14 July 2023
    U.S. release
    8 December 2023, through GKIDS
    Also won
    A Golden Globe and two Annie Awards

    Source: AniTrendz

  3. 18 July 2025

    Infinity Castle becomes the biggest anime film

    鬼滅の刃 無限城編 (Mugenjō-hen)

    Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle opened in Japan on 18 July 2025 and reached ¥10 billion faster than Mugen Train had. After 31 days it stood fourth on Japan's all-time chart. By late September it had passed US$555 million worldwide, the highest total for any anime film.

    Key facts
    Studio
    ufotable
    After 31 days
    ¥25.8 billion and 18.27 million tickets in Japan
    Worldwide
    More than US$555 million by 22 September 2025
    Note
    Totals kept growing after this date

    Sources: Cartoon Brew, Cartoon Brew
